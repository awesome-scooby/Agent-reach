## 2. What exactly does Rule B say today (2027 handbook)? Overseas bachelor? Master's as the qualifying degree?

### Takeaway
**Under the current 2027 rules, Rule B needs a completed bachelor's degree "AQF Level 7 or overseas equivalent", in any discipline, with no recency condition.** An overseas bachelor's is expressly accepted if UNE assesses it as equivalent. A master's degree is *not* listed as a Rule B basis for the BPsychSc. Postgraduate degrees are listed under Rule A, which is the full 144 cp, 24-unit degree. So the applicant's Rule B eligibility rests on the 2016 Pakistani B.E. being assessed as AQF 7 equivalent. In contrast, the GradDip's own Rule B accepts an AQF 8+ qualification from someone without a bachelor's.

### Cited Findings
- **2027 handbook (BPSYSC, version 2027.05, effective 2027-01-01, data updated 2026-11-04), verbatim:** "This course is offered under two admission rules, each with its own Course Structure (Program of Study). Rule A: You have completed, at minimum, an Australian Year 12 qualification or overseas equivalent… Rule B: You have completed a Bachelor qualification (AQF Level 7 or overseas equivalent) in any discipline. If you are admitted on this basis, you will be eligible for admission under Rule B." — [UNE Handbook 2027, BPSYSC](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/BPSYSC)
- **Previous Higher Education entry text (2027), verbatim:**
  - "Rule A: You have undertaken any of the following qualifications with an appropriate Grade Point Average (GPA): Undergraduate Certificates; Diplomas; Advanced Diplomas/Associate degrees; Bachelor degrees (if you have completed a Bachelor degree please see Rule B); Postgraduate degrees… Rule B: You have completed a Bachelor qualification (AQF Level 7 or overseas equivalent) in any discipline."
  - Postgraduate degrees therefore map to Rule A (full degree), not Rule B. — [UNE Handbook 2027, BPSYSC](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/BPSYSC)
- **Supporting document for Rule B:** "A certified academic transcript of your relevant Bachelor qualification." — [UNE Handbook 2027, BPSYSC](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/BPSYSC)
- **Block credit is automatic:** "Some entry rules come with Advanced Standing or Articulation Programs that are assessed automatically without the need for you to fill in an additional application… If you are admitted under Rule B, you will be granted 72 credit points of Block Advanced Standing." — [UNE Handbook 2027, BPSYSC](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/BPSYSC)
- **Further credit:** "If you are admitted under Rule B, you can apply for up to a further 24 credit points of Advanced Standing, including the qualification on which admission was based." — [UNE Handbook 2027, BPSYSC](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/BPSYSC)
- **The Rule B wording has been identical since at least the 2023 handbook** ("Rule B: You have completed a Bachelor qualification (AQF Level 7 or overseas equivalent) in any discipline"), and the 2026 text is the same. — [UNE Handbook 2023](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2023/BPSYSC); [2026](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2026/BPSYSC)
- **The 2021 and 2022 handbooks** had no rule text in the handbook entry. They said only "A candidate shall be qualified for admission (see Admission, Credit and Enrolment Guidelines)." — [UNE Handbook 2021, BPSYSC](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2021/BPSYSC); [2022](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2022/BPSYSC)
- **Rule B period of candidature, 2027:** "(a) two years as a full-time candidate; (b) up to six years as a part-time candidate." The 2026 handbook said "up to five years" part-time. — [UNE Handbook 2027](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/BPSYSC); [2026](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2026/BPSYSC)
- **GradDip admission rules (2027), for contrast:** "Rule A: You have completed a Bachelor qualification (AQF Level 7 or overseas equivalent) in any discipline… Rule B: You do not hold a Bachelor qualification, but have completed a qualification which is a minimum of AQF Level 8 (or overseas equivalent) in any discipline." — [UNE Handbook 2027, GDPSY](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/GDPSY)

### Inferences
- **The applicant's 2020 La Trobe Master of Engineering Management (AQF 9) cannot itself be the Rule B basis for the BPsychSc**, because Rule B names a "Bachelor qualification" only. It could support Rule A admission, but Rule A is the full 24-unit degree. It *would* satisfy the GradDip, but the applicant enters the GradDip under its Rule A via the bachelor's anyway.
- **The Pakistani B.E. (completed 2016) must be accepted as "overseas equivalent" to AQF 7.** Four-year Pakistani engineering bachelor's degrees are commonly treated as comparable to an Australian bachelor's, and La Trobe's admission to a master's suggests it was accepted as a bachelor's. However, UNE makes its own assessment. This is an inference, not a verified UNE determination.

## 3. Is there a recency (10-year) rule in the current rules? How is it measured? Is a 2016 bachelor's caught? Would a 2020 master's satisfy it instead?

### Takeaway
**No recency rule appears in the 2027 (or 2023–2026) handbook Rule B admission rule or in its block-credit statement.** "Ten years" appears in four other places, none of which is the Rule B admission test:
- (a) credit for *individual units*;
- (b) the GradDip's credit rule;
- (c) Honours entry (recency of the *psychology* sequence, which can be waived);
- (d) UNE's *general* advanced-standing guidelines ("previous studies must be less than 10 years old").

Point (d) is the residual risk. If UNE applied it to Rule B block credit, a 2016 bachelor's would be over 10 years old at any 2027 start date. The handbook presents Rule B block credit as automatic and attached to the admission rule, which argues against this. **The applicant should get written confirmation from UNE (AskUNE/Future Students) before applying.** The 2020 master's cannot substitute, because Rule B requires a bachelor's.

### Cited Findings
- **BPSYSC 2027, individual-unit credit only:** "If you are admitted into this course and believe you have already completed the equivalent of one or more of the units in your Course Structure (Program of Study) within the last ten years, you can apply for Individual Unit Advanced Standing." This sits under a heading separate from "Course Entry Advanced Standing", which grants the Rule B block. — [UNE Handbook 2027, BPSYSC](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/BPSYSC)
- **GDPSY 2027:** "You cannot apply for Advanced Standing for units completed more than ten years ago." This applies to credit, not admission. — [UNE Handbook 2027, GDPSY](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/GDPSY)
- **Honours (HBPSYC) 2027:** "You have completed your APAC-accredited sequence of units in Psychology within the past ten years.*" and "* Ten-Year Rule Waivers: You can apply for a waiver of the ten-year rule at application if you can demonstrate relevant work experience or studies within the past ten years." — [UNE Handbook 2027, HBPSYC](https://handbook.une.edu.au/courses/2027/HBPSYC)
- **UNE graduate-entry page [snippet, not verbatim-verified]:** graduate entry is for people whose bachelor's "did not include an accredited psychology major/sequence; or was undertaken more than 10 years ago, in which case the psychology major/sequence would not be recognised for entry into a fourth year psychology program". The same page says the course is "a 24 unit degree" and Rule B entrants complete "12 units (72cp)" with "12 units (72cp) in block advanced standing". — [UNE Graduate Entry into Psychology](https://www.une.edu.au/about-une/faculty-of-medicine-and-health/school-of-psychology/future-students/graduate-entry-psychology)
- **UNE general Advanced Standing Application Guidelines [snippet]:** "previous studies must be less than 10 years old (there are a few exceptions to this such as mathematics)". — [UNE Advanced Standing Application Guidelines](https://www.une.edu.au/study/applying-to-une/transfer-and-advanced-standing/advanced-standing-application-guidelines)
- **UNE's maximum advanced standing page [snippet]** notes that individual course rules may impose additional limits. — [UNE Maximum Advanced Standing](https://www.une.edu.au/study/applying-to-une/transfer-and-advanced-standing/maximum-advance-standing)

### Inferences
- **The "older UNE FAQ" ten-year statement most likely referred to one of three things:**
  - (i) the recency of a *prior psychology* sequence (a graduate whose psychology major is over 10 years old must redo it, which is why graduate entry exists);
  - (ii) UNE's general credit-currency rule;
  - (iii) wording from before 2023 that is no longer in the handbook.

  None of these, as currently written, makes a 2016 non-psychology bachelor's ineligible for Rule B admission.
- **How a 10-year limit would be measured, if UNE applied one:** UNE wording is "within the last ten years", relative to the application/credit assessment. No document found defines the measurement (completion date to commencement date versus to application date).
  - If completion was in 2016 (any month), by Feb 2027 (T1) it is more than 10 years in almost all cases. At the extreme, a December 2016 completion is 10 years and about 2 months old by T1 start.
  - T2 (June 2027) and T3 (Oct 2027) starts are clearly over 10 years.
  - An application lodged before the 10-year anniversary is the only scenario in which a 2016 degree could arguably be "within ten years".
- **The 2020 master's would be within any 10-year window**, but it does not meet the Rule B "Bachelor qualification" condition. It is therefore not a workaround for the BPsychSc.
- **For the GradDip, recency of the prior degree is irrelevant.** Its admission text has no recency rule, and the applicant would not be claiming credit, so the GradDip carries no 10-year exposure.
- **Honours recency is irrelevant for this applicant**, because the psychology sequence would be freshly completed (2028–2029).

### Gaps
- The text of the "older UNE FAQ" the applicant saw was not located. Its date and wording are unknown.
- No UNE source explicitly states whether the general 10-year credit-currency guideline applies to admission-rule block advanced standing. **This is the single most important item for the applicant to confirm with UNE in writing.**

## 4. Units remaining after Rule B, minimum part-time duration alongside full-time work, intensives, placement

### Takeaway
**Twelve units remain, all of them PSYC units:**
- 10 core units: PSYC101, 102, 200, 202, 206, 213, 363, 366, 371, 372.
- 2 listed electives.

**Delivery:** All are available online with online-supervised exams. No core unit has a mandatory intensive (PSYC202 has an optional online intensive). There is no placement.

**Duration:** UNE's own part-time Rule B plan (2 units per trimester in T1 and T2 only) finishes at the end of T2 in year 3 (about September 2029 for a February 2027 start). Using T3-only listed units can bring the finish forward to T1 2029. The full-time plan takes 2 years.

### Inferences
- **Placement:** no placement or unpaid-leave block is needed for the BPsychSc Rule B units. The applicant's unpaid-leave plan matters only later (Honours intensives are online; Master's placements come after that).
- **Weekly load:** 2 units per trimester is about 24–30 study hours per week on top of a 38-hour job (based on UNE's 12–15 h per unit per week).
- **Fastest realistic part-time sequence for a T1 2027 start** (my own construction, checked against 2027 offerings and prerequisites):
  - T1-27: 101 + 102
  - T2-27: 200 + 213 (or 202 + 206)
  - T3-27: PSYC315 (listed)
  - T1-28: 202 + 206
  - T2-28: 363 + 366 (or 371 + 372)
  - T3-28: PSYC309 (listed)
  - T1-29: 371 + 372

  This finishes T1 2029 (about May 2029) instead of T2 2029. Finishing by T2 2028 would need at least one 4-unit trimester, which is the full-time pattern.
- **A T2 or T3 2027 start** (June/Oct 2027) delays completion. 200-level units are not offered in T3. A T2-2027 start with 2 units per trimester most likely finishes T2 2029 to T1 2030.

### Gaps

- It is not confirmed whether UNE will publish 2027 Rule B T2/T3 commencement plans.
- Whether a part-time student may take 3 units in some trimesters is a choice, not a rule (the handbook says candidates "can each year choose to study less units than the full-time pattern or more units than the part-time pattern").

## 5. 2027 CSP band and per-unit cost; total for 12 units; CSP eligibility with prior bachelor's and master's; HECS-HELP

### Takeaway
**Cost per unit depends on the funding band:**

| Band | 2027 rate per EFTSL | Per 6 cp unit (0.125 EFTSL) |
|---|---|---|
| Band 2 (professional pathway psychology) | $9,880 | $1,235 |
| Band 4 (other behavioural science) | $18,025 | $2,253 |

**Course totals:**
- **BPsychSc Rule B:** PSYC units in an approved professional-pathway bachelor's course are charged at Band 2. 12 units = **$14,820 at 2027 rates**, or roughly $15,400 if later years are indexed by about 3.6% a year.
- **GradDip:** a graduate diploma is not eligible for professional-pathway rates (Department criterion: bachelor or bachelor-honours degree), so it should be Band 4. 10 units = **$22,531 at 2027 rates**, or roughly $23,200 spread over 2027–2029.

**Eligibility:** As far as found, there is no rule barring CSP for a second or third qualification. The Student Learning Entitlement (7 EFTSL) counts only CSP units with census dates on or after 1 Jan 2022, so the applicant's 2016 and 2020 studies do not reduce it.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 and 2027 maximum student contribution per EFTSL (University of Sydney summary of Commonwealth amounts):**
  - Band 1: $4,738 (2026) / $4,908 (2027)
  - Band 2: $9,537 / $9,880
  - Band 3: $13,558 / $14,046
  - Band 4: $17,399 / $18,025

## 8. Does completing Rule B BPsychSc or the GradDip qualify for UNE's fourth year? Entry requirements, mode, intensives, CSP, intake

### Takeaway
**Yes. Both routes meet the qualification limb of UNE Bachelor of Psychology with Honours (HBPSYC) Rule A.** Rule A requires a bachelor's with an APAC sequence, or a bachelor's plus a graduate diploma with an APAC sequence.

**Entry is competitive:**
- APAC sequence completed within 10 years.
- WAM of at least 75 across eight named core units.
- A quota applies (the recent cut-off was "low Distinction").

**Format:** online only; T1 intake only; applications close 31 October the year before; 1 year full-time or up to 2 years part-time; CSP only. There are mandatory *online* intensive schools: PSYC421, 422, 423 and the thesis (a 3-day online intensive plus a 1-day online poster day). No placement is listed.
