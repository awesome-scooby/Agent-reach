# Income and job market: Cert IV mental health, provisional and registered psychologists, and engineering tendering alternatives (Perth and Melbourne, as of 25 Nov 2026)

Applicant baseline used in the inferences: electrical project engineer, Perth, $57/h x 38 h x 52 weeks = **$112,632 gross**, plus 12% super (about $13,516), so about **$126,148 total package**, plus paid annual leave, personal leave and public holidays.

How reliable each source is:
- **Primary documents I fetched and read myself:** FWO SCHADS pay guide (published 24 Jun 2026), MBS Online item pages, WA Department of Education salary page, APS fee page, Psychology Board fees page, APS NDIS price-limit page, Robert Walters 2026 Salary Guide PDF, Healthcare Australia 2026 guide, APS InPsych 2022 survey article, APS April 2026 media release, McCullough Robertson summary of the SCHADS decision, and SEEK salary pages.
- **Job-ad figures:** these came from search-engine summaries of listing pages. They are marked "(ad, via search)" and should be read as advertised ranges, not verified pay. Exact listing URLs and open/closed status were not always confirmable.

## A. Cert IV in Mental Health jobs: roles, SCHADS pay from 1 July 2026, advertised rates, shift fit, remote options

### Takeaway
SCHADS (social and community services stream) minimums from 1 July 2026 run from **$36.22/h (Level 2.1) to $50.25/h (Level 4.4)** for full-time and part-time staff. Casual rates run from **$45.28 to $62.81/h**, and casual Saturday/Sunday rates from about **$63 to $113/h**. Perth and Melbourne ads for Cert IV support work mostly sit in the **low $40s to about $50/h casual**. A full-time Cert IV job would pay roughly **$72k to $92k a year**, well below the current $112.6k. Casual weekend shifts can fit around an 8am to 4pm job and pay more per hour than the applicant's base rate, but only as a top-up. Evidence for fully remote or telehealth Cert IV roles is thin. Lifeline crisis-line roles are mostly **volunteer**.

- **Side income.** One 8-hour casual Saturday shift a week for 48 weeks:
  - Level 2.1 ($63.39/h): about $507 a shift, about **$24.3k a year** gross.

- **Fit around an 8am to 4pm job.** Weekend, sleepover and overnight shifts plainly exist (Friday/Saturday overnights in Perth, weekend roles in Melbourne). Some employers (e.g., some HelpingMinds roles) offer weekday business hours only. Evening (after 4pm) "afternoon shift" work is paid at a modest loading (e.g., L2.1 $40.75/h, or $49.80/h casual).
- **Peer work** is gated by lived experience, not the Cert IV. Lifeline-type crisis work is mainly volunteer, so it is a way to gain experience, not an income stream.

## B. Provisional psychologists: public, school, NGO, private and telehealth pay

### Takeaway
Provisional psychologists earn roughly **$72k to $94k base in public and school roles**. The best-documented Perth figure is the WA Department of Education's **$93,706 (Level 1.1)** for school psychologists with provisional registration. Private and NGO salaries for provisionals and new graduates mostly run **$70k to $90k** (new-graduate private-sector guide: WA $90k, VIC $88k). A few telehealth or remote employers do hire provisionals (e.g., MedHealth remote, $77k base). In most scenarios a provisional psychologist earns **less than the current $112.6k**.

### Inferences
- Among the sourced options, WA school psychology ($93.7k provisional, then $108.8k at general registration, plus 8 weeks of school-holiday leave and 12% super) is the strongest-paid **employed provisional** pathway in Perth. It is still about $19k below the current base until general registration, then about $4k below.
- A provisional psychologist cannot run a Medicare-billing private telehealth practice. Telehealth during the provisional period means salaried or sessional work under an employer with supervision. Private billing at about $200/session is only realistic after general registration.

### Gaps

- Victorian Grade 1 (provisional) 2026 rate not verified.
- No sessional or hourly rates found for provisionals at named telehealth platforms.

## C. Registered psychologists (general registration, no clinical endorsement): salaries, contractor splits, telehealth pay, fees, Medicare, workload

### Takeaway
- **Employed:** about $95k to $124k in Victorian public roles (PK1 to PL3), $108.8k to $137.4k in WA schools, and a private-sector average of about $110k (VIC) to $120k (WA), plus super.
- **Contractor and telehealth:** advertised pay is about **$100 to $200 per attended session**. The APS suggested fee is **$330** for 2026–27, and the AAPi recommends $340.
- **Medicare:** the Better Access rebate for a registered psychologist (item 80110 or video 91170, 50+ min) is **$101.55**. A clinical psychologist's (80010) is **$149.05**. Both are capped at **10 individual sessions per calendar year**.
- **Workload:** APS data show private practitioners average about **23 paid plus 17 unpaid hours a week**.

### Inferences
These are illustrative calculations from the sourced inputs. They assume a $200 fee, 44 working weeks a year (an assumption, not sourced) and 10% lost to no-shows and late cancellations, and they are before costs and tax.

**Sole-trader telehealth gross revenue:**
- 15 sessions a week: about **$118.8k**
- 20 sessions a week: about **$158.4k**
- 25 sessions a week: about **$198.0k**

Against these, the sole trader must fund practice costs (section D), their own super (12% of $112.6k is about $13.5k to match the current package), unpaid leave (already reflected in the 44-week assumption) and income protection. Per the APS data, 20 sessions a week is close to a full-time load once about 17 unbillable hours are added.

**Contractor at 20 attended-equivalent sessions a week, 44 weeks, $200 fee, 10% lost:**
- 60% split: about **$95.0k**
- 65% split: about **$103.0k**
- 70% split: about **$110.9k**
- All are generally without super or leave unless written into the contract. Roughly equal to, or below, the current base salary.

**Medicare gap at $200.** A $200 fee leaves a gap of about **$98.45** after the $101.55 rebate. At the APS $330, the gap is $228.45. A $200 fee therefore positions the practice well below the APS and AAPi guides, which helps demand. The 10-session annual cap limits Medicare-funded episodes per client.

**Medicare telehealth vs face-to-face.** Better Access video sessions (91170) attract exactly the same rebate as in-person sessions (80110). That supports a work-from-home telehealth model financially.

### Gaps
- No APS or Psychology Board published guidance stating "20 to 25 client hours a week is sustainable full-time". The closest data is the APS's 23 paid-hours average.
- No authoritative survey of contractor percentage splits (the brief's "55 to 70%" is not verified).
- No data on actual weeks worked per year by private psychologists.
- No named telehealth platforms (e.g., Lysn, Mind Plus, etc.) with published employed sessional rates.
- Typical gap fees charged in 2026: no survey found. The $180 to $280 market range is from a non-authoritative consumer guide.

## D. Private practice costs in 2026

### Takeaway
Fixed costs for a solo telehealth practice are modest:
- Ahpra registration **$493/yr**
- Professional indemnity (PI) about **$380 to $580/yr** bundled with public liability
- Psychology Today **$24.95/month** after a 6-month free period
- Practice software from **free (Halaxy core) to about $30 to $60/month**
- CPD **30 h/yr including 10 h peer consultation** (current standard; revision under consultation)

The bigger costs are unfunded super, leave and income protection, plus time spent on supervision and CPD.

### Cited Findings
**Ahpra and the Psychology Board:**
- General registration renewal **$493** nationally ($492 NSW). Provisional registration $493. General registration application fee $604. The fee schedule is effective **1 Oct 2026**. [Psychology Board fees](https://www.psychologyboard.gov.au/Registration/Fees.aspx)
- The previous fee was $473 for 2025/26 (registration period 1 Dec 2025 to 30 Nov 2026). [Ahpra 18 Sep 2025](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/News/2025-09-18-fees-announcement-2025.aspx)

**Professional indemnity insurance:**

## F. Demand for psychologists, telehealth saturation, and Medicare telehealth rule changes

### Takeaway
Demand is strongly documented:
- The Government's **Psychology Supply and Demand Study (14 Apr 2026)** found access to psychology in health settings was **57.3% short in 2025**, projected to reach **96.6% by 2038**.
- In 2022, one in three psychologists could not take new clients, and average waits were 55 days.

Telehealth remains fully rebated under Better Access: video 50+ min = same $101.55 benefit as in person. The 1 Nov 2025 redesign tightened **referral** rules (the referral must come from the patient's MyMedicare practice or usual GP) and **GP** telehealth for mental health treatment plans (established clinical relationship). I found **no established-relationship requirement on the psychologist video items themselves**. I found no data on telehealth market saturation.

### Cited Findings
**Supply and demand study (released by the Department of Health, Disability and Ageing, 14 Apr 2026):**
- Access shortfall in health settings: **57.3%** in 2025, **96.6%** by 2038.
- The APS says numbers are growing but average hours are falling.
- APS President: the problem is partly "a system that isn't using psychologists effectively".
- Sources: [APS media release](https://psychology.org.au/about-us/news-and-media/media-releases/2026/urgent-action-required-following-release-of-psycho); [Government compendium report Apr 2026](https://hwd.health.gov.au/resources/primary/psychology-compendium-report-april-2026.pdf)
- An AAPi summary of the same study (via search): a current shortfall growing to over 7,000 psychologists by 2038. Including unmet demand, the shortage in health was over 10,000 in 2025, rising to over 24,000 by 2038. [AAPi](https://aapi.org.au/Web/About-AAPi/News/Articles/2026/April/PsychologySupplyandDemandStudyreleased.aspx)
- Macquarie University commentary (May 2026): "shortfall to hit 96% by 2038". It also notes the Government has directed the Psychology Board to move to a single integrated five-year training program from 2026. [Macquarie Lighthouse](https://lighthouse.mq.edu.au/article/2026/may-2026/demand-for-psychologists-to-outstrip-supply-by-96-in-2038)

**Waitlists (APS Feb 2022 survey):**
- 33% unable to take new clients, up from 22% in Jun 2021 and about 1% pre-pandemic.
- Average waits of 55 days among those still taking clients.
- Waitlists held by 79.2% of regional and 73.5% of metro psychologists.
- Source: [APS InPsych](https://psychology.org.au/for-members/publications/inpsych/2022/winter-2022/psychologists-unpaid,-underfunded-and-overworked)

**Medicare telehealth and referral rules:**
- Item 80110's notes require referral by a GP or prescribed medical practitioner at the patient's MyMedicare practice, or by their usual medical practitioner. A psychiatrist or paediatrician can also refer. [MBS 80110](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?q=80110)
- From 1 Nov 2025, GPs can only provide telehealth for new mental health treatment plans if they are the patient's MyMedicare practice or usual GP *and* have an established clinical relationship. [Health Dept redesign PDF](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/better-access-redesign-from-1-november-2025_0.pdf); [RACGP](https://www.racgp.org.au/advocacy/advocacy-resources/changes-to-mbs-mental-health-items-gps)
- The APS reports the Government confirmed that MyMedicare registration would not be mandatory for Better Access referrals, and a "usual GP" can refer. [APS](https://psychology.org.au/insights/federal-government-responds-to-aps-advocacy-and-so)
- The MBS 91169 page shows no established-clinical-relationship or location requirement for individual psychologist video items. The location restrictions (MMM 4 to 7, 15 km) apply only to *group* video items. [MBS 91169](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?q=91169)
- The APS March 2026 submission says the referral requirements add administrative burden for psychologists. [APS submission Mar 2026](https://psychology.org.au/getmedia/2792b2e4-50e5-495f-bcc1-fa7f4eab9364/20260327-APS-submission-RRR-Medicare-access-funding_WEB.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Demand.** The documented shortage supports good demand for a Perth-based telehealth psychologist at a sub-APS fee such as $200, particularly for Medicare-referred clients.
- **Referral-flow risk.** Clients now need a referral from their usual GP or MyMedicare practice, and GPs need an established relationship to do new mental health plans by telehealth. Client acquisition therefore depends on GP referral networks more than before.
- **Saturation risk.** Telehealth lets clients choose any psychologist nationally, so a solo practitioner competes nationally on fee and niche. This is reasoning; no data was found.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on **telehealth market saturation** or the share of Better Access sessions delivered by telehealth in 2026.
- No 2025 or 2026 survey of current psychologist wait times; the latest found is 2022.
- No verified figures on regional differences in demand between Perth and Melbourne.
- The full text of the 1 Nov 2025 redesign and the March 2026 fact sheet was not read; the details above come from item notes and secondary summaries.
