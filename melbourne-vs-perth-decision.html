Corrections to version 1

Six things in the previous version were wrong. They are listed here rather than quietly fixed, because two of them reversed conclusions.

1 · "Perth is 27% dearer than Melbourne" — composition error, now corrected

That compared all-dwellings medians. Melbourne's housing stock is far more apartment-heavy, which drags its median down. Like-for-like as at 31 Aug 2026: houses — Perth ~$938k vs Melbourne $920k (+2%); units — Perth $675k vs Melbourne $629k (+7%). Perth is modestly dearer, not dramatically. The original premise ("Perth is the cheap option") is still out of date, but the magnitude was overstated by roughly ten times.

2 · Superannuation was missing entirely from a 20-year wealth model

At 12% SG on ~$213k combined, super adds an estimated $1.56m by 2046. Every net-worth figure in v1 was understated by roughly that amount. It is broadly city-neutral, so rankings held — but the absolute numbers were wrong.

3 · No capital gains tax was applied — and it reverses the timing conclusion

v1 let the rent-and-invest scenarios compound a share portfolio tax-free while the house they compete against is CGT-exempt as a main residence. Applying CGT (50% discount, 37% marginal) costs Scenario D $157,620. Buying now (A, $3.88m) now beats renting-then-buying-2029 (D, $3.85m). v1's "wait until 2027–28" recommendation does not survive this correction.

4 · LMI was treated as unavoidable — you likely qualify for the First Home Guarantee

Income caps and place limits on the First Home Guarantee were removed on 1 Oct 2025. Price caps are Melbourne $950,000 and Perth $850,000. A 5% deposit with no LMI is available, which v1 did not model at all.

5 · A Perth jobs figure was estimated but presented as data

v1's job-market chart showed Perth "~+41,000 jobs". That was derived from a growth forecast, not measured, and sat beside a genuine ABS figure for Victoria. It has been removed. Victoria's net debt, shown unsourced as "~24%", is confirmed at 24.9% of GSP (2026–27 Budget).

6 · Melbourne's commute penalty was overstated; Craigieburn was mispriced

v1 said a Melbourne house at this budget means a 50–80 minute commute. Werribee, at a $510,000 median, is ~32 minutes by train to Southern Cross — comparable to Armadale's 39 minutes in Perth. Craigieburn was listed as in-budget at "~$620k"; more recent data puts it near $705,000.

Housing supply and risk factors

Melbourne

Supply & risk
  • Large outer-corridor land supply in Melton, Wyndham and Whittlesea — caps price growth in exactly the sub-$650k band, but keeps entry affordable.
  • Victoria's land tax threshold is $50,000 (cut from $300,000 in 2024, legislated to 30 Jun 2033). Principal residence is exempt, but this materially suppresses investor demand.
  • Apartment stock has been a poor store of value — roughly flat over a decade.
  • Vacancy 1.4–1.6%: tight, but the least tight of the two.

Perth

Supply & risk
  • 0.6% vacancy, below 1.5% for 18 consecutive months — severe rental scarcity, supportive of prices, punishing for renters.
  • Construction capacity is the binding constraint — trades are competing with mining, energy and transmission projects for the same workforce.
  • Historical precedent for a long flat period: Perth's median fell between 2010 and 2020 after the last mining boom unwound.
  • Entry-level corridor has largely been absorbed — see section 10.

Industry structure

Melbourne — diversified

Broad base, lower concentration risk
  • Strong: healthcare & social assistance, professional services, finance, education (largest university sector in the country), IT/software, logistics, advanced manufacturing.
  • Data centres: 37 projects in the pipeline, 38 operating — roughly a quarter of Australia's ~6 GW national pipeline; a state action plan targeting up to $25bn.
  • Deepest switchgear and switchboard manufacturing base in Australia.
  • Weakness: softest labour market of the two right now, and a heavy state debt load constraining future public spending.

Perth — concentrated

Stronger now, narrower base
  • Strong: mining & resources, energy, construction, transmission/grid, defence (Henderson), healthcare.
  • Funded infrastructure: $973m Western Power network investment; $1.5bn Powering WA Clean Energy Fund; Clean Energy Link–North (354 km, Malaga to Three Springs).
  • Data centres: 7 pipeline, 14 operating. Largest is CDC Maddington (200 MW, in construction).
  • Weakness: concentration risk. A commodity downturn transmits quickly into employment, housing and state revenue. Many senior resources roles assume FIFO.
Industry concentration is the structural difference. Perth's economy is stronger today and more exposed to a single cycle; Melbourne's is weaker today and more diversified. Neither of those is a verdict — they are different risk profiles, and which one suits you depends on whether your own occupation is inside or outside the resources chain.

If you will drive to work, Perth is easier; if you will take public transport from an inner or middle suburb, Melbourne is better served. At the outer edge of either city, both are hard.

Taxes & government charges

Income tax is federal and identical in both states

Australian personal income tax, the Medicare levy and the Medicare levy surcharge are set by the Commonwealth and do not vary by state. On the same salary you pay the same income tax in Melbourne and Perth. Only state taxes and charges differ — and payroll tax is paid by employers, not by you.

Victoria's $50,000 land tax threshold deserves attention if you might ever buy an investment property. It was cut from $300,000 in 2024 as a COVID debt measure and is legislated to stay until 30 June 2033. WA's threshold is $300,000. The finding that changed between versions

The previous version claimed Melbourne at this budget meant a 50–80 minute commute. That was wrong. Werribee, at $510,000, is about 32 minutes by train — better than Armadale in Perth and only nine minutes behind Gosnells, while costing $70,000–$100,000 less than the Perth equivalents.

The accurate statement is narrower: Melbourne's cheapest houses are cheaper than Perth's cheapest houses, and the commute difference is smaller than assumed — but Perth's road traffic around those suburbs is materially lighter, which matters if you drive rather than take the train.

Correcting for CGT reverses version 1's timing advice

v1 concluded that renting and buying in 2029 (Scenario D) produced the most wealth. The margin is thin, and D still beats B comfortably, but "wait two years" is no longer supported.

Climate and insurance

Both cities face rising insurance costs, though from different drivers. Melbourne's exposure is concentrated in riverine and flash flooding and in bushfire at the peri-urban fringe (the Dandenongs, Yarra Valley and outer north-east). Perth's is concentrated in extreme heat, bushfire on the eastern and northern fringes, and long-run water security in a drying south-west climate — Perth's rainfall has declined materially since the 1970s and the city now depends heavily on desalination. Which city suits which priorities

Melbourne is likely to suit you if…

Stated as conditions, not a recommendation
  • Your occupation sits outside the resources chain — health, education, professional services, IT, finance, diversified engineering.
  • You will use public transport rather than drive.
  • You want the lowest entry price for a freestanding house with rail access — Werribee at $510k has no Perth equivalent.
  • You value food, arts, sport and a large city, and can tolerate grey weather.
  • You use GPs often and want bulk-billing.
  • You want lower exposure to a single industry cycle and can accept a softer job market now.
  • Being 2–3 hours from other major cities matters to you.

Perth is likely to suit you if…

Stated as conditions, not a recommendation
  • Your occupation is in or adjacent to resources, energy, construction or transmission.
  • You need work quickly — the labour market is measurably tighter.
  • You will drive to work and want lighter traffic and shorter distances.
  • Sunshine, heat and beach access materially affect your wellbeing — this is the largest measured difference between the two cities.
  • You prefer a smaller, less dense city.
  • You may later buy an investment property — the $300k land tax threshold against Victoria's $50k is a real long-run difference.
  • Isolation from the east coast does not bother you, or is a positive.
No overall score is given, deliberately. A single number would require weighting sunshine against bulk-billing and commute time against industry diversification — and those weights are yours, not the document's. The two clearest measured differences are climate (Perth: 3,200 sunshine hours against 2,383; 138.7 clear days against 48.6) and economic diversification (Victoria broad but indebted; WA concentrated but solvent). Most other categories are closer than their reputations suggest.

Section 15 · Personal recommendation · This is opinion, not evidence · Applies to one household only

For an electrical project engineer ($113k) and an AHPRA-registered psychologist (~$100k), $160k savings, both first home buyers, children expected within four years, freestanding house required, commute matters

Melbourne — and buy when you're ready, not in 2028.

Confidence 65%, down from 72% in version 1. The direction survived every correction; the margin narrowed. Two findings drove the change: the price gap between the cities is 2% on houses rather than 27%, and cost of living is a tie rather than a Perth advantage. What is left supporting Melbourne is narrower but still holds.

  1. Werribee resolves the objection that nearly flipped this. A $510,000 house, 32 minutes by train to Southern Cross — cheaper than anything comparable in Perth and a shorter commute than Armadale. Version 1's claim that Melbourne meant a 70-minute commute was simply wrong, and it was the strongest argument against Melbourne.
  2. You are buying a house, which removes Melbourne's biggest asset-quality risk. Melbourne apartments have gone nowhere for a decade. Melbourne houses grew 91–96%. Since a freestanding house is non-negotiable for you, that risk does not apply — and Melton at +16% over the year shows the outer corridor is moving.
  3. Tiger's specific field is deeper in Melbourne, and it is non-FIFO. 37 data centre projects against 7, roughly a quarter of the national pipeline, a $25bn state action plan, and the country's deepest switchboard manufacturing base. Perth's electrical work is real and well funded, but ruling out FIFO removes a disproportionate share of its senior roles.
  4. Cycle position still favours Melbourne, on a narrower margin. Melbourne sits ~6.3% below its Nov 2025 peak; Perth is coming off +15.6% and has turned negative. Perth needs roughly a full percentage point more growth every year for twenty years just to match Melbourne's outcome from here. Caveat: this rests on the growth differential, which is the single softest input in the model.
  5. Do not use the $100,000 deposit plan. It pays ~$14,000 of LMI to sit between two better options. Either 20% ($120,000, $2,987/month, no LMI) or the First Home Guarantee at 5% ($30,000 down, no LMI, $126,500 kept liquid). With a baby coming, the buffer is worth more than the lower repayment — so the FHG option is defensible even though it costs $560 more a month.
What changed about timing. Version 1 said wait until 2027–2028. Applying CGT to the rent-and-invest scenarios removes that advantage — buying now ($3.88m) narrowly beats renting and buying in 2029 ($3.85m). Buy when you have a Melbourne job and a deposit you are comfortable with. Do not try to time the bottom; the forecasters disagree by 15 percentage points on this year alone.

The honest case against this recommendation

Perth has the better job market today (4.0% against 4.8% unemployment), vastly better weather on every measured axis, lighter traffic, your existing friendships, and a state government with a third of Victoria's debt burden. Cost of living is a tie. House prices are within 2%.

If you weight sunshine, existing relationships and near-term job security at the top of their plausible range, Perth is a defensible choice and not a mistake. That is why confidence is 65% and not 85%.

The one fact that would flip this

Whether Tiger can secure a Melbourne offer at $115k+ within three months.

Victoria added 26,000 jobs in the year to March 2026 — the weakest since 2021, against +71,500 the year before. WA runs 4.0% unemployment with employers competing for exactly his skillset. Every argument above assumes he lands a role promptly.

Make it testable before you commit anything. Apply for Melbourne roles from Perth now. A written offer at $115k+ converts this from a forecast into a fact. If twelve weeks of genuine applications produce nothing at his level, buy in Perth — and note that on the corrected numbers, Perth is a far closer second than version 1 suggested.
