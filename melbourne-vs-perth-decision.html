Three facts that break the framing

Your brief assumed Perth is the affordable, job-rich option and Melbourne is the expensive indulgence. As of September 2026, two of those three assumptions are no longer true.

Assumption inverted

Perth is now dearer than Melbourne

$999,987 vs $786,718

Cotality median dwelling values, 31 Aug 2026. Perth sits 27% above Melbourne. Perth rose 15.6% over the year; Melbourne peaked in Nov 2025 and is ~6.3% below that peak. Melbourne is now the cheapest mainland east-coast capital and the cheapest big capital in the country.

Window closed

Perth's entry corridor has been eaten

$710k–$755k

The suburbs that were Perth's first-home-buyer answer — Byford, Baldivis, Ellenbrook — are now above your budget. At $600–650k what remains is Armadale / Gosnells / Midland. In Melbourne, $600–650k still buys in Craigieburn, Werribee or Melton — and those prices are flat-to-falling, not post-boom.

Macro reversed

Rates are rising, not falling

4.35% cash rate

The RBA hiked three times in 2026 (Feb, Mar, May). All four major banks now forecast another hike before year-end; Westpac has modelled a 4.85% peak. National values have fallen five months straight, 3.6% below the March 2026 peak. Any model assuming a 2026 rate-cut tailwind is wrong.

Why this changes the decision. At $600k you now pay the same stamp duty ($0, both states, as first home buyers) and the same repayment in either city. Price is not the differentiator. What differs is what $600k buys relative to each city's own market — 76% of Melbourne's median versus 60% of Perth's — and which market you are buying at the top of.

Tiger — job suitability index

weight 20%

Not "how many electrical engineer ads exist" — how many roles genuinely match a 31-year-old project engineer with 3+ years in switchboards and switchrooms, who will not do FIFO.

The no-FIFO constraint costs Perth its top quartile

Perth's headline engineering salaries beat Melbourne's — but a disproportionate share of the $170k+ non-graduate electrical roles in WA are resources roles that assume FIFO or long-term site residency. Ruling out FIFO removes most of the top of the WA salary distribution while leaving Melbourne's largely intact, because Melbourne's high-paying electrical work is metro data centres and metro infrastructure.

This is the single biggest correction to the intuition that "Perth pays engineers more."

Property growth potential

weight 15%

You asked not to extrapolate the last five years. Doing so would be the single worst error available here — because the last five years are exactly what makes Perth look good and Melbourne look bad, and they are the least repeatable five years in either city's record.

Since 2000, Perth has beaten Melbourne — in two bursts, with a lost decade between

Total growth 2000 → 2026
  • Perth +459% (~6.0–6.8% CAGR) · Melbourne +346% (~4.9–5.9% CAGR)
  • But Perth's entire outperformance was delivered in two mining-linked bursts: 2003–2008 and 2020–2026.
  • Between them, Perth's median fell from 2010 to 2020 — a full decade of negative real growth after the last boom unwound.
  • Melbourne's path was slower but far steadier, with shallower drawdowns.

Where each city sits in its cycle right now

As at 31 August 2026
  • Perth: +15.6% year-on-year, fastest capital — but just rolled over. Houses −3.0% and units −4.1% over the three months to August. Slipped back under $1m.
  • Melbourne: peaked November 2025 at ~$840k, now $786,718 — roughly 6.3% below peak, with the cheapest entry of any large capital.
  • Vacancy: Perth 0.6% (below 1.5% for 18 straight months) vs Melbourne 1.6%. Perth's rental market is tighter — supportive of prices, punishing if you rent.
  • Migration: WA net interstate +9,800 (best in over a decade); Victoria −3,200. But Melbourne takes a far larger share of net overseas migration, which is the bigger number nationally.
The honest counter-argument for Perth. Migration and vacancy both currently favour Perth, and WA's fiscal position is far stronger (net debt 7.1% of GSP, eighth consecutive surplus, $3.5bn). If WA's energy and critical-minerals build-out is structural rather than cyclical, Perth's growth need not mean-revert. That is the bull case, and it is not silly — it is the reason confidence here is 72% and not 90%.

Be sceptical of Scenario D, even though it wins

Renting in Melbourne and buying in 2029 produces the highest modelled net worth ($2.44m) — but it wins on assumptions you cannot control. It needs (a) Melbourne prices to actually dip in 2027 as modelled, (b) 7.2% investment returns to hold, and (c) three years of genuine savings discipline while renting with a newborn. The case against waiting — stated fairly

This entire timing recommendation rests on Melbourne prices being flat-to-down through 2027. That is the majority view right now (ANZ −9.2%, CBA −7.0%, Westpac −4.0% for 2026), but KPMG and most independent forecasters expect +4% to +7% instead. Reasonable compromise: treat 2027–2028 as a target window rather than a firm delay, and buy the moment Tiger has a signed Melbourne contract and you clear $123,500 in cash. Do not wait for a price bottom you cannot identify in advance.

Job-market strength, side by side

How to hold both facts at once. Perth is easier to get a job in today (4.0% vs 4.8% unemployment, +1.75% vs +1.25% forecast employment growth). Melbourne has far more of the work Tiger specifically wants over the next fifteen years (37 vs 7 data centre projects, ~25% of the national 6 GW pipeline, a $25bn state action plan, and the country's deepest switchboard manufacturing base). You are choosing between a better next six months and a better next fifteen years. The model weights the fifteen years — but only because your buffer can absorb the six months, which is why the deposit structure matters so much.

If we were Tiger and Tigress

We would buy in Melbourne — in 2027 or 2028, at 80% LVR, and not before Tiger has a signed contract.

  1. Perth is no longer the cheap option — it is 27% dearer than Melbourne ($999,987 vs $786,718), and your budget no longer reaches Byford, Baldivis or Ellenbrook. The reason Perth looked obvious has already expired.
  2. You would be buying Perth at the top and Melbourne near the bottom. Perth is +15.6% year-on-year and just turned negative; Melbourne is 6.3% below its Nov 2025 peak. Perth needs ~1 percentage point more growth every year for twenty years just to draw level.
  3. Melbourne has the career Tiger actually wants. 37 data centre projects to Perth's 7, a $25bn state pipeline, the deepest switchboard manufacturing base in the country — and all of it non-FIFO, which is exactly the constraint that removes Perth's top-paying roles.
  4. The answer does not depend on liking Melbourne. Remove lifestyle from the model entirely and Melbourne still wins 77.5 to 62.2. Modelled 2046 net worth: $2.32m Melbourne against $1.72m Perth.
  5. Structure beats city choice on the risks that could actually hurt you. A $123,500 deposit instead of $100,000 gets you to 80% LVR, kills $8,800 of LMI, and extends your unemployment survival from six months to twelve. Do that before you worry about which suburb.

The one fact that would flip this to Perth

Whether Tiger can secure a Melbourne offer at $115k+ within three months.

Everything above assumes he can. Victoria added just 26,000 jobs in the year to March 2026 — the weakest since 2021, against +71,500 the year before — while WA runs 4.0% unemployment with employers competing for exactly his skillset. If a genuine Melbourne search returns nothing at his level in twelve weeks, the wealth model is irrelevant: an unemployed engineer with a Melton mortgage and a newborn is a materially worse outcome than a well-paid one in Armadale.

So make it testable, and make it free. Apply for Melbourne roles now, from Perth, before committing to anything. A written offer at $115k+ converts this from a forecast into a fact — and if three months of genuine applications produce nothing, buy in Perth and revisit in five years. The second fact worth watching: if Perth's growth holds above 5.5% CAGR while Melbourne stays under 4%, the property case inverts too.

Method and honesty notes. This is a scenario model, not a forecast. Property values from 2027 onward are estimates under stated assumptions and should not be read as predictions. The single most influential input is the Melbourne-vs-Perth growth differential (5.0% vs 3.5%); equalising it at 5.0% narrows the 2046 gap from ~$602k to ~$120k, so treat the direction as robust and the magnitude as soft. WA concessional stamp duty above $600k is estimated by linear phase-in to $800k and should be confirmed with RevenueWA. Job-board counts are deliberately not used as demand measures. Not financial advice — check the mortgage structure with a broker and the duty figures with each state revenue office before acting.
