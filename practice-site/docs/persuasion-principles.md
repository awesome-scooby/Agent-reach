# Psychology in the copy: what we use and what we rule out

The aim is to make booking **easy and unthreatening** for someone tired or anxious on a phone. We won't pressure anyone into booking. Psychology regulation backs this up: under section 133 of the National Law and Ahpra's advertising guidance, an ad for a regulated health service must not mislead, use testimonials, create unreasonable expectations of benefit, or encourage unnecessary use of services. These principles have not had legal review; the practitioner should check them against current Ahpra guidance before launch.

## Used (ethical behavioural design)

| Principle | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| Recognising yourself: visitors see their own situation in plain words | "People often get in touch when…", with no diagnostic labels |
| Normalising: reaching out is ordinary | "You don't need to know exactly what's wrong before you get in touch." "If you're not sure… that's very common." |
| Reducing uncertainty: the unknown is the biggest barrier | Fee, duration and cancellation terms shown upfront. A three-step "What happens next" section. A first-appointment explainer on the Fees page |
| Fewer decisions | One primary action per section. Short paragraphs. The action keeps the same name everywhere ("Book an appointment") |
| Autonomy: visitors stay in control | "At your own pace." "You decide how often you come, and you can pause or stop at any time." |
| Warmth from a real person | First-person introduction, and an approved real portrait (or an intentional text-only layout) |
| Being honest about the next step | The intake status sits next to the button. "An enquiry doesn't book an appointment." |
| The button where the decision is made | The booking action appears in the hero, after "What happens next", and on every service page, plus an Appointments button in the header |
| Safety | Urgent-help wording on every page, so nobody waits on a routine enquiry in a crisis |

## Ruled out

| Tactic | Why |
|---|---|
| Testimonials, reviews, star ratings | Not allowed in health advertising under the National Law |
| Scarcity or urgency ("only 2 spots left", countdowns) | Pressures an anxious audience and risks encouraging unnecessary use. A *true* waitlist status is shown plainly instead |
| Outcome promises ("feel better in 6 sessions") | Creates unreasonable expectations |
| Fear-based copy, exit pop-ups, pre-ticked consent | Manipulative, and it erodes trust |
| Discounts or free first sessions without clear terms | Ahpra caution about inducements. Only offered if she chooses to, with terms stated |
| Retargeting pixels, session replay | Privacy risk for health-adjacent browsing. Off by default |

## How we'll judge it (if measurement is approved)
We'll count booking-link clicks in aggregate and keep them separate from confirmed appointments. No individual tracking.
