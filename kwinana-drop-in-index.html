02 — Racket sports, answered properly

Where you'll actually get a hit

You asked not to be sent to court-hire listings. Here is the straight answer for each of the four you named.

Tennis — yes, three genuine options, one of them 5 minutes away

Kwinana Tennis Club runs organised social tennis on Monday evenings and Thursday mornings in mixed-doubles format, explicitly "accessible for all levels", plus a Thursday evening round robin for intermediate/advanced. Mixed doubles social = partners rotate; you arrive alone and the session allocates you. Book online through ClubSpark or pay cash on the day. This is your single best proximity play at roughly 3 km.

Rockingham Tennis Club is the weekend answer: Saturday 1:30–4:00pm, play from 2pm, $5 for non-members (free to members). Eight courts, four lit. Mon/Wed/Fri 8:30am socials too at $8 non-member.

Melville Palmyra is the most structured: timed 22-minute sets run by a Set Captain who rotates the draw, so you play four different sets with four different combinations. $10 non-member. Worth the 28-minute drive if you want the rotation done for you.

Pickleball — the cheapest organised racket sport near you, and it runs Sunday

Baldivis Indoor Sports Complex (13 min) runs organised games Wednesday 8:30–10:30am and Sunday 4:30–6:30pm at $12, pay on arrival, plus casual sessions Tuesday and Friday 9:30–11:30am at $5.50. Pickleball's social format is rotational by default — you queue, you're paired, you rotate off. It is the most beginner-forgiving racket sport on this list and the Sunday slot fits your schedule exactly. Kung Fu Paddle also runs Saturday afternoon sessions at Cockburn ARC.

Padel — solves the partner problem by design, but it's the priciest

Padel 360 Cockburn (Yangebup, ~15 min) runs Padel Socials: 1.5-hour sessions where you sign up as an individual and the club builds the matches — three games, then a coffee together. That is exactly the format you asked for. The catch: padel court economics are expensive, and Padel 360 does not publish social pricing publicly. Expect $25–40 per head based on comparable Perth clubs. Confirm before booking.

Badminton — the weak link. Be honest about this one.

Near Kwinana, badminton is mostly court hire, which is the exact model you said you don't want. SSBA in Armadale charges $16.50/court/hour for non-members — you need to bring three people. Baldivis Badminton Club at Baldivis Indoor Sports Complex does advertise social play for all levels and is only 13 minutes away, but publishes no times or prices; call 9591 0840 before assuming. The genuinely social badminton scene in Perth sits north and east (Dianella, Willetton, Parkwood), which is a 35–50 minute drive. Recommendation: don't lead with badminton. Put pickleball in that slot instead — same rotational social feel, quarter the price, twenty minutes closer.

07 — Read this before you commit

Four things that will trip you up

1. Seasonality will break half this list

Touch football at Tompkins Park is summer only. Hiking clubs stop running Perth day walks from November to March — South of the Border explicitly pauses and resumes March/April. Rockingham Triathlon Club's series runs October to April. Conversely, social tennis and outdoor sport are at their best right now (September). Build the plan you can run this quarter, not a generic year-round one.

2. "Social" at a tennis club is not the same as "social" at a drop-in

Club social tennis assumes you'll join eventually. You'll get two or three visitor sessions of genuine welcome, then a gentle conversation about membership. That's fine and membership is usually cheap, but don't plan to freeload for six months. Drop-ins (parkrun, pickleball casual, Casual Football Co, bouldering) have no such expectation at all.

3. The Cockburn ARC team-fee maths is per team, not per player

$81.80 per game is the team fee. Split across 8–10 players that's roughly $8–11 each — genuinely cheap. But if your team is short one week, the fee doesn't shrink. Going through Just Play, which places individuals into teams that need bodies, is how you avoid ever carrying that.

4. The single highest-yield thing you can do is the cheapest

Two parkruns sit inside 15 minutes of you, both free, both 8:00am Saturday, both with coffee afterwards — and the coffee is the actual point. It costs nothing, needs no booking, no equipment, no partner, and you'll see the same faces weekly, which is the mechanism by which strangers become people you know. Start there in week one regardless of which plan you pick.
