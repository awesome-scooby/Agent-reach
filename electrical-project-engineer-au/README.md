# Electrical Project Engineer — Australian Delivery Program

A progressive, practical program to take you from "I understand electrical engineering"
to "I can confidently deliver an electrical project as a Project Engineer."

**Scope chosen:** switchboards/MCCs, commercial buildings, industrial/mining/water, and
utilities/substations/renewables.
**Starting level:** fundamentals up.
**Target roles:** office/project engineering + site delivery + commissioning/testing +
client/consultant coordination (the full PE spectrum).

---

## How this program works

- One module at a time. Each module ends with exercises and a quiz.
- Answer the quiz in chat. Wrong answers get explained, re-taught and re-tested at
  slightly higher difficulty before moving on.
- Every concept is framed as: *a drawing, an email or a site issue lands on your desk —
  what do you do next?*

## Standards discipline used throughout

This program **names standards and explains when they become relevant**. It does not
reproduce clause text or invent clause numbers. Any specific limit, table value or clause
must be verified against the **current edition** of the standard and the **project
specification**, which may be more onerous.

Always keep these five buckets separate in your head:

| Bucket | Example | Who enforces it | Can it be waived? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal (Act/Regulation) | State electrical safety legislation, WHS Regs, licensing | Regulator / inspector | No |
| Australian Standard | AS/NZS 3000, AS/NZS 61439 | Called up by law, contract, or both | Only via the pathway the standard/law allows |
| Client specification | "All MCCs to be Form 4b Type 6" | Client / superintendent | Yes — by client, in writing |
| Project specification | Consultant's Section 26 spec, drawings | Consultant / superintendent | Yes — by RFI/variation |
| Manufacturer requirement | Torque settings, ventilation, derating | Warranty and type-test validity | No, without vendor sign-off |

When these conflict: **the most onerous applies**, unless the client formally agrees
otherwise. Get it in writing. "The consultant said it on a call" is not a record.

## Learning pathway

### Level 1 — Electrical fundamentals for delivery
Voltages, three-phase, kW/kVA/PF, the three currents, fault level, earthing and the MEN,
volt drop, maximum demand and diversity, and the "chain of ratings" mental model.
**Objective:** be able to sanity-check any number on any electrical document.

### Level 2 — Reading Single Line Diagrams
Every symbol, every rating, and how an experienced PE interrogates an SLD.
Utility → MSB → MCC → DB → motor, built up step by step.
**Objective:** review an SLD and produce a credible list of technical queries.

### Level 3 — Schematics and control circuits
Power vs control, 24 VDC loops, DOL/VSD starters, E-stops, interlocks, interposing
relays, DI/DO/AI/AO, protection trips, feedback.
**Objective:** trace any wire on a control schematic and explain why it exists.

### Level 4 — Cabling and termination
Cable selection and sizing, volt drop, short-circuit withstand, cable types, glands,
lugs, ferrules, shields, segregation, tray/ladder/conduit, bend radius, IP ratings.
**Objective:** review a cable schedule against an SLD and find the errors.

### Level 5 — Switchboards and equipment
Design → manufacture → FAT → delivery → install → terminate → SAT → commission →
handover. Forms of separation, busbars, CTs, metering, interlocks, ratings.
**Objective:** know exactly what to check at each gate, and what to reject.

### Level 6 — Testing and commissioning
Pre-commissioning through energisation to handover. Every practical test: what, why,
how, expected result, common failure, and the PE's action.
**Objective:** run a commissioning campaign and read test results critically.

### Level 7 — Project delivery
Deliverables, design reviews, RFIs, submittals, procurement, ITPs, hold and witness
points, NCRs, defects, as-builts, handover, variations, programme, risk.
**Objective:** run the delivery machinery without being told what to do next.

### Level 8 — Real-world PE scenarios
Undersized cables, mismatched terminations, motors that won't start, rejected shop
drawings, site termination defects, FAT readiness, and many more.

### Level 9 — Advanced troubleshooting and coordination
Discrimination failures, harmonics, earthing faults, nuisance tripping, VSD problems,
protection maloperation, multi-party coordination under programme pressure.

### Final — Electrical Project Engineer delivery simulation
A full project package: SLD, equipment data, cable schedule, terminations, RFIs, test
results, supplier and programme problems. You act as the PE. Assessed on technical
understanding, drawing interpretation, problem solving, safety, communication,
coordination, QA/QC, commissioning, commercial awareness and risk identification.
