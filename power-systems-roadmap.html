How to actually run this Built for an ADHD brain, not a disciplined one

Rule 1

Read the banner, not the page

The sticky bar at the top names one task. That is the whole instruction. Scrolling the full roadmap to “plan” is avoidance wearing a hard hat.

Rule 2

45 minutes is the hard ceiling

Long tasks are pre-split into sessions. Set a timer, stop when it rings, even mid-sentence. Stopping while it still feels good is what makes you come back.

Rule 3

There is no streak

Every task is self-contained and resumable cold. Miss three weeks and you lose nothing — the plan has an order, not a calendar. Just reopen and read the banner.

Rule 4

Tick the box the moment you stop

Partial credit beats accurate credit. An unticked half-done task is the thing that makes you avoid opening this page tomorrow. Tick it, move on.

Rule 5

Boring modules get the 25-minute treatment

Standards and rules reading is the module people quit on. Do it in 25-minute blocks with a 5-minute break, and only ever right after something you enjoyed.

Rule 6

Build tasks over read tasks, always

If motivation is low, skip ahead to any build task in the current phase. Order matters less than momentum. Come back for the reading later.

The honest read

Why this track, and where it can go wrong

Why it works: Victoria is rebuilding its transmission network and firming capacity at the same time, and the constraint is people who can run and defend connection studies. Your project engineering background is not wasted — owner-side and consultancy roles specifically want engineers who have stood on a site and managed a contractor, provided you can also open PSS®E.

Where it goes wrong: people learn the tools and never produce an artefact. A hiring manager cannot see a completed course. They can read a twenty-page study report with your name on it. Phase 4 is the phase that gets you paid — the first three only make it possible.

The shortcut worth taking: if a consultancy offers a lateral move at Phase 2 pay, take it. Learning Phase 3 on someone else’s licence, with a senior reviewing your work, is worth more than six months of solo evenings.

The trap: chasing every certification. Chartered status pays. A stack of short-course certificates does not.

Assumptions. 340 hours across twelve months at 5–8 hours per week, averaging 6.5. Session counts assume a 45-minute working block. Hour estimates are for someone with an accredited electrical engineering degree who has not used it for analysis in three years — if the fundamentals come back faster than expected, Phase 1 compresses.
