⚡ The single most valuable fact in this document

WA's Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991, reg. 36, requires that the applicant, or at least one person in the employ of the applicant, holds a current WA Electrician's Licence. ⚠️ The nuance that matters for your decade

The threat to you is not that AI does your job. It is that AI removes the bottom rungs — the graduate who used to do take-offs, mark up drawings, chase RFIs and compile O&M manuals. Those tasks are genuinely going. What rises in value is the opposite set: signing off, walking the site, carrying liability, negotiating a variation, deciding what the model got wrong. Every one of those is an experience-gated, licence-gated, or relationship-gated task. Your strategy should be to move up that stack as fast as possible and to never again compete on tasks a graduate with a good model could do.

💰 Money play — sell the productivity, don't donate it

If AI lets you produce a tender in 3 hours that used to take 12, an employer captures 100% of that gain and your salary does not move — the 8% Energy & Resources premium is the proof. A client paying you a fixed fee per tender captures none of it. Same work, same output, and your effective rate goes from $140/hr to $450/hr. This is the entire economic argument for building a service business rather than chasing an AI-skills pay rise inside your current job.

🔴 Should you become a surveyor? No.

Your electrical engineering degree earns you almost no credit toward a Bachelor of Surveying — the overlap is first-year maths and a little computing. Curtin runs the only comprehensive surveying honours degree in WA, it is four years, and cadastral registration then requires a further two-year Professional Training Agreement with five submitted survey projects, a Survey Law exam, three practical field exams and a Board interview.

You would surrender $130k a year to study, re-enter at $85k, and reach licensed-surveyor money around age 38 — roughly what you would earn as an Electrical Project Manager at 34 with no study at all. The statutory monopoly is real and the practice-ownership ceiling is genuinely high, but you are paying six years and a career reset to buy it. Do not pursue.

🟡 Is quantity surveying better for you? As a skill, yes. As a career change, no.

QS averages ~$80k in Perth. Changing careers into it is a $50k pay cut to learn measurement and contract administration — most of which you would absorb anyway by moving into estimating or contracts administration in your own discipline, at your current salary, with no tuition.

But the qualification has one genuinely valuable use, and it is not a job. See the depreciation play below.

💰 Money play — the tax depreciation monopoly

This is the single legitimate "surveyors make a lot of money" story, and almost nobody outside the profession knows it. The ATO accepts construction-cost estimates for capital works deductions from a very short list of qualified professionals — and quantity surveyors are the primary one. Property investors need a depreciation schedule for essentially every investment property.

A schedule sells for $600–900, takes a competent QS 2–3 hours once templated, and every investor who buys a property needs one exactly once. The work is repeatable, remote, weekend-compatible, and protected by a professional-body requirement rather than by skill. Ten schedules a month is $72–108k/year of side revenue. This — not employment as a QS — is the reason to consider an AIQS-accredited Graduate Diploma, and it is the only reason.

💰 Money play — sell survey services without ever being a surveyor

Only cadastral work is legally reserved. Engineering survey, setout, as-builts, volumes, reality capture and scan-to-BIM are completely unregulated. A drone plus a terrestrial scanner is a $25–60k capital purchase that bills $1,200–2,500/day for as-built capture and progress documentation, and construction clients want it monthly. You can own that business, employ or subcontract the operator, and never sit a Survey Law exam. If surveying attracts you commercially, buy the revenue — do not buy the degree.

💰 Money play — the builder registration shortcut

The WA Building Services Board runs five registration pathways, not one. The standard route is the CPC50220 Diploma plus seven years' supervisory experience, but a separate pathway exists for people holding a relevant degree or membership of a professional building institution with five years' experience. WA electrical licensing — what you can and can't do

This is the part of your brief with the most widely believed misinformation, so I'll be blunt: there is no accelerated, RPL, or degree-recognition pathway from an electrical engineering degree to a WA electrician's licence. None. Your degree is worth zero here. And it does not matter, for the reason in the green box.

What you can legally do today

  • Design electrical installations, systems and switchboards
  • Specify, estimate, procure and project-manage electrical work
  • Supervise and direct licensed electricians on a project basis
  • Witness and manage testing and commissioning — the licensed person performs the work
  • Non-contact inspection: thermography, condition monitoring, audits
  • Extra-low-voltage work (below 50 V AC / 120 V DC) — controls, instrumentation, comms
  • Own and direct an EC-licensed electrical contracting business

What you cannot do

  • Any electrical installation work on low-voltage installations — full stop
  • Disconnect or reconnect fixed wiring, including "just" an isolator
  • Test-and-tag or issue an electrical safety certificate in your own name
  • Advertise or contract for electrical work without an EC licence
  • Obtain a restricted electrical licence — the REL requires a trade qualification (plumber, refrigeration mechanic, fitter). An engineering degree does not qualify. This door is closed.

🔴 The apprenticeship reality, so you can stop wondering

A WA electrician's licence requires a four-year apprenticeship with a licensed employer, a Certificate III in Electrotechnology Electrician (UEE30820), and a Capstone Assessment. There is no mature-age fast track and no degree credit. As an apprentice you would earn roughly $25–55k a year for four years — a cumulative income loss of well over $350,000 against your current salary — to obtain a licence whose ceiling as a working electrician is below what you already earn. The Electrician's Training Licence and worker permits you may have read about exist for apprentices and for overseas-qualified tradespeople completing gap training, not for engineers. Do not do this.

💰 Money play — hire the licence, own the company

WA's Electricity (Licensing) Regulations, reg. 36, requires that the EC licence applicant or at least one person in the applicant's employ holds a current WA Electrician's Licence, and that this person has completed the Electrical Contractor's Training Programme modules covering Operation (Electrical) Legislative Requirements and Electrical Requirements. The nominee can be an employee. You, the owner, need no electrical licence at all.

The practical shape: you hold the client relationships, do the estimating and tendering (your genuine edge), run the projects and own 100% of the equity. Your nominated licensed electrician — a good A-grade sparky costs $120–150k plus a package — carries the technical licence and signs the compliance certificates. Cost to establish: EC licence application, the nominee's ECTP modules, PI and public liability insurance, and working capital. Total under $40k against a four-year apprenticeship costing you $350k+ in foregone income. This is the arbitrage. Verify the current requirements with Building and Energy before you commit, and never perform or direct unlicensed electrical work — the whole point of this structure is that it is legitimate.

1 High-income engineer — the safe baseline

Senior Engineer → Electrical Project Manager ($165k Perth average) → Engineering/Project Manager → Engineering Manager ($220–300k) → principal consultant or contractor. Zero study beyond tickets and CPEng. Zero capital. Very low risk.

Accelerate it by moving into a constrained speciality: HV protection and control, BESS and grid connection, or data centre electrical. Four years at Curtin (income collapses to casual), two-year Professional Training Agreement, five cadastral projects, Survey Law exam, three field exams, Board interview. 4 Quantity surveying

Grad Dip part-time while working (no lost income), then a $20k pay cut to enter as a QS, recovering by year 4–5, then Senior QS and Commercial Manager ($200–270k). Respectable but strictly slower than Path 2 to the same destination. Take the qualification for the depreciation business; skip the job.

5 Property developer

You are renting with no equity, so this cannot start now — and anyone telling you otherwise is selling a course. Sequence: income first, then deposit, then a first owner-occupier purchase, then equity, then a first small development around year 5–7.

Your edge when you get there is real: with construction and estimating capability you project-manage your own builds and capture the 15–22% builder's margin other developers pay away. Focus on Perth middle-ring subdivision and duplex sites in R30–R60 zones near Metronet corridors. Very high risk, very high reward, and it should be funded by Path 8, not attempted instead of it.

6 Registered builder

CPC50220 (or an alternative BSB pathway your degree may satisfy) plus 5–7 years documented supervisory experience, then BP00 and BC00 registration. Realistically year 6–8 from today. Building companies are capital-hungry, cash-flow-brutal and insolvency-prone — WA has watched a lot of them fail. Worth holding as an option once Path 8 is running, because an electrical contracting business teaches you the trade-management skills a builder needs, at far lower risk.

7 Medicine

Covered fully in Parts 6 and 7. Entry gate realistically closed at UWA (68.5 average GAMSAT for interview), tight at Notre Dame, GPA dependent on how GEMSAS treats your overseas bachelor's. Financial break-even around age 62 against Path 1 and never against Path 8. Pursue only as a vocation.

The one-page answer

"What exact path should I take?"

Stay an electrical engineer, and change three things about how you sell it.

One — move the salary. Within six months, take an Electrical Project Manager or estimating role at a contractor or head contractor, ideally in a constrained speciality: HV protection and control, BESS and grid connection, or data centres. That is $165–185k without a dollar of tuition, and it is the single highest-ROI action available to you. Everything else is funded by it.

Two — sell the same skill directly on weekends. Freelance electrical estimating and switchboard design, starting within a month, at $110–180/hr, moving to fixed fee per tender by month six. Six to eight hours a Saturday. This satisfies your value-over-hours preference exactly, and unlike a second job it accumulates clients, reputation and pricing power.

Three — convert it into a licensed entity around year three. Employ a licensed A-grade electrician as your EC nominee. You never need an electrician's licence, an apprenticeship, a surveying degree, a QS qualification or a medical degree. You need one good sparky on the payroll and the estimating capability you already have. From there the business funds the property, the property funds itself, and by year ten you own something you can sell.

Do not study surveying. Do not retrain as a QS to work as one. Do not start an apprenticeship. Do not do medicine unless you want to be a doctor for its own sake — and if you genuinely do, email GEMSAS about your GPA before anything else, because that one answer determines whether the question is even live.
