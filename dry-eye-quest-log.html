Morning ritual

~15 min · do it in this order

This is the only part where the order actually matters. Heat melts the oil, massage pushes it out, cleaning clears the exit, drops go on last so you're not washing them straight off.

Make it happen when your brain won't
  • Stack it onto something you already do. Mask goes on the second you sit down with coffee, or while the shower runs. Never as its own free-floating task.
  • Keep the mask where you'll be, not where it belongs. On the desk, on the pillow, next to the kettle. Out of a drawer.
  • Ten minutes is a video, not a wait. Podcast, an episode, a phone call — eyes shut, ears busy.
  • Buy a second mask. One in the wash is the single most common reason this routine dies in week two.
Skip rule: short on time? Heat and massage. Those two are the treatment; everything else is support.

Screen hours

All day · damage control

Screens don't dry your eyes by glowing at you. They cut your blink rate by roughly two thirds, and most of the blinks you do manage never fully close — so the oil never gets spread and the tear film evaporates. Everything here is about blinking properly and losing less water.

Screen settings that genuinely help (and one that doesn't)
  • Brightness matched to the room — a screen brighter than its surroundings makes you stare. Dim it until it stops feeling like a light source.
  • Bigger text, fewer squints. Squinting is a blink you didn't take. Zoom to 110–125% and stop leaning in.
  • Kill the glare. Window at 90° to the screen, never behind it or behind you.
  • Blue-light glasses are the one that doesn't. The evidence doesn't support them for dry eye or eye strain. The blink rate is the problem, not the wavelength.
Evening screens count too. Phone, TV and gaming are the same load on the same eyes — the drill applies after work as well.

Wind-down

Last hour · ~12 min

Overnight is when the surface either recovers or gets worse. If you wake up with eyes stuck or stinging, this block is the one to fix first.

Bedroom check: no fan or aircon pointed at the bed, and if the room runs dry, a humidifier does more overnight than any drop.

The kit

2 sorted · 3 gaps

You already have the two that matter most. The gaps below are what usually decides whether the clinic work holds or you're back in six months.

Sorted Heated eye mask Yours works. Two things: wash the cover weekly, and buy a second one so laundry day never breaks the streak. If it's cool by minute five, reheat mid-session.
Sorted Preservative-free drops Right call — preserved drops used four times a day irritate the surface they're meant to soothe. Next bottle, look for a lipid or oil-containing formula: your problem is the oil layer, not the water.
Gap · get first Lid cleanser Sterile lid wipes, a lid lotion on a cotton pad, or a hypochlorous acid spray. Baby shampoo is the old advice and it's out of favour — it strips the oil you're trying to keep.
Gap · slow burn Omega-3 Aim for roughly 1,000–2,000 mg combined EPA + DHA daily, triglyceride form. Honest framing: the big trial (DREAM) failed to beat placebo, but it's low-risk and many clinics still back it. Three months before you judge it. Check with your GP if you're on blood thinners.
Gap · if rooms run dry Hygrometer, then maybe a humidifier Measure before you buy. Under 40% humidity at the desk means the room is pulling water off your eyes all day — that's a humidifier's job, not a drop's.
Don't buy "Redness relief" drops Vasoconstrictors (tetrahydrozoline, naphazoline) whiten the eye by squeezing blood vessels and cause rebound redness when you stop. Nothing to do with dry eye. Skip preserved drops for frequent use too.

Desk geometry

Set once · pays out daily

The single highest-leverage change for an all-day screen user: get the screen lower. Gazing downward covers more of the eye surface, so less of it is exposed to evaporate.

Screen height top edge ≤ eye level
If you use a laptop all day, a stand plus a separate keyboard is the fix. Otherwise you're looking up into it with your eyes wide open.
Distance 50–70 cm
Close screens pull you into a fixed stare. Further back, bigger text.
Air nothing at your face
Vents, fans, heaters, car blowers. Redirect all of them.

What to expect, honestly

So you don't quit at week three
Week 1 Some relief from the expression itself, and it may partly fade. That's normal and not a failure. The glands were just emptied; they need to start producing thin, clean oil again.
Weeks 2–4 Mornings get easier before evenings do. Gritty afternoons on heavy screen days are still expected.
Weeks 6–12 This is when to judge whether the routine is working. Gland tissue turns over slowly — judging at week two tells you nothing.
Ongoing IPL is usually a course of 3–4 sessions, then top-ups every 6–12 months. Book the follow-up your optometrist recommended — home care extends the result, it doesn't replace the course.
Forever MGD is managed, not cured. The day you stop the heat is the day the glands start backing up again. Once a day, indefinitely, is the realistic finish line.

Stop the routine and get seen

  • Actual pain rather than grittiness, or new sensitivity to light
  • Any change in vision that doesn't clear when you blink
  • A red eye that stays red, discharge, or a swollen lump on the lid
  • Getting steadily worse after four to six weeks of doing this properly
