    Do not buy yet

    • Walking pad / treadmill ($150–400). Buy it after 30 logged days, not before. Bought first, it becomes furniture.
    • Smartwatch or fitness tracker. Measuring is not moving. Your phone already counts steps.
    • Gym membership. Adds a 25-minute commute to a 2-minute habit. Wrong order.
    • Merino anything. $90 for socks. Polyester base layers work at a quarter of the price; you are walking around the block, not summiting.

    Why two minutes and not twenty

    Starting costs almost everything; continuing costs almost nothing. A 20-minute plan asks you to pay the full price up front, in advance, while sitting down — which is exactly when you have the least to spend.

    So the target here is not fitness. It is never having a zero day. Two minutes, most days, for months, beats a heroic 45-minute session you do twice and then abandon in February.

    The heat number above is built on this: showing up is worth about five times as much as duration. A 2-minute day and a 15-minute day are not far apart. A 2-minute day and a skipped day are miles apart.

    It's cold and dark outside

    Dress for 10°F / 5°C warmer than it is. The standard mistake is dressing for how cold it feels at the door. Four minutes in, you're generating heat and over-dressed, which is its own kind of miserable.

    The first three minutes are the worst ones. They are always the worst ones. Cold stops being the problem right about when you'd have turned back.

    Go out for the light, not the exercise. In winter, 10 minutes of outdoor daylight before noon does more for your sleep and mood than the walk does for your legs. Cloudy still counts — it's an order of magnitude brighter outside than in your living room.

    Shrink the loop. "Go for a walk" is a project. "To the end of the street and back" is four minutes and requires no decisions.

    I broke the chain and want to quit

    Missing once is an accident. Missing twice in a row is the start of the new pattern — so the only rule that matters is never miss two days back to back. Not "be consistent." Just: don't stack them.

    After a gap, do not "make it up." Making it up means a punishing session that teaches you this whole thing hurts. Go back to two minutes. Today's square looks identical either way.

    Nothing is happening and I feel stupid

    At two minutes a day, nothing visible will happen for weeks. That is expected and it is not the point yet. What you are building right now is the identity of someone who moves daily — the volume can be raised later in about a week. The habit takes months.

    Things that do show up early, before any visible change: falling asleep faster, less afternoon slump, less low-back stiffness from sitting. Watch for those instead of the mirror.
