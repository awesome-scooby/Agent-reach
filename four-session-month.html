Why your real voice is the whole strategy

In July 2025 YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to inauthentic content, explicitly targeting mass-produced and templated videos. In January 2026 it ran its largest enforcement wave yet against AI-generated channels. AI-assisted content is still fully monetisable — what gets killed is content that looks machine-stamped: identical structure, synthetic narration, stock slideshows.

Your setup is on the safe side of that line by construction:

  • Real human narration (not text-to-speech)
  • Real screen recordings of you actually doing the thing
  • An original result or opinion in every video
  • Structure that varies because the task varies

Use AI for research, scripting, editing, thumbnails, and packaging — all fine. Just don't let it hold the microphone. And toggle the "altered or synthetic content" disclosure in YouTube Studio when you use realistic AI-generated visuals.

How to name the channel (and what not to do)

Pick a name that describes the outcome, not you. It should survive you changing your mind about format. Two or three words, easy to spell out loud, handle available on YouTube and one social.

Don't use your own name (kills resale value and pins you to one topic), don't put "AI" and "Hub"/"Lab"/"Central" together, and don't spend more than 45 minutes on this. The name is worth roughly 2% of your outcome; the 48 videos are worth the other 98%.

04

The four-session month

This is the centrepiece. Don't make one video a week — that means task-switching seven times per video. Instead do one mode per session, and finish four videos a month.

Week1
SCRIPT DAY · 3 hrs
Pick 4 topics from your idea list. AI drafts 4 scripts from your locked template; you rewrite the hooks and the opinions. Out: 4 finished scripts.
Week2
RECORD DAY · 3 hrs
Mic on, same setup, four in a row. Voice + screen capture. Don't re-record for small stumbles — you cut them later. Out: 4 raw recordings.
Week3
EDIT DAY · 4 hrs
Text-based editing (Descript or similar): delete words, video follows. Same template on all four. One hour each, hard stop. Out: 4 exported videos.
Week4
PACKAGE DAY · 3 hrs
Thumbnails, titles, descriptions, chapters, affiliate links. Cut 8 Shorts from the four transcripts. Schedule everything. Out: a month published, in advance.

Make it stick

Put four recurring calendar events in now, named by mode — "SCRIPT DAY", not "YouTube stuff". Same day of the week, same time, same 3-hour block. A vague intention to "work on the channel" is how this dies in month two.

What to do when you miss a session (you will)

Never try to catch up. Catching up is how a missed week becomes a missed quarter. Instead, shrink the month: miss script day, ship 2 videos instead of 4. The rhythm survives; the volume flexes.

The one session you never skip is PACKAGE DAY, because unpublished finished videos earn exactly nothing and sit there generating guilt.

Non-negotiables

  • No intro animation. Ever.
  • No "hey guys, welcome back"
  • Cut every pause, "so", and "um" — the edit tool does this in one pass
  • Titles are search queries, not clever
  • Thumbnail: 3 words maximum

Title patterns that work

  • "How I [outcome] with [tool]"
  • "[Tool] vs [Tool] for [specific use]"
  • "Stop doing [task] manually"
  • "I automated [task]. Here's what broke."
Where the affiliate money actually goes

Link placement beats link volume. Put the tool link first line of the description and say once, out loud, mid-video, "link's in the description." That's it. No link dumps.

Disclosure is not optional in Australia. The ACCC requires clear disclosure of commercial relationships — say "these are affiliate links" in the video and write it in the description. Also flip YouTube's paid promotion toggle where a brand relationship exists. This costs you nothing in conversion and protects you completely.

06

Twelve months, four phases

Each phase has one job and one gate. Don't judge yourself on anything except the gate.

Phase 0 · Weeks 1–2 — Setup

Job: exist. Channel created, tax admin done, first video live.

Gate: one video published. That's the whole bar.

Do not build automation. Do not buy a course. Do not design a logo for three days.

Phase 1 · Months 1–3 — Find the format

Job: publish 12 videos and find out which format the algorithm likes from you.

Watch only two numbers: click-through rate (aim >4%) and average view duration (aim >50%). Ignore subscribers entirely. Subs are a lagging indicator and watching them daily is pure self-harm.

Gate at month 3: has any single video passed 1,000 views? If yes, make four more like it. If no, change the format — not the niche.

Phase 2 · Months 4–6 — Double down + first dollars

Job: make more of whatever worked. Apply to the Partner Program at 500 subs + 3,000 watch hours (early access tier). Add affiliate links to your top 3 videos and every new one.

Gate at month 6: 500+ subscribers and at least one affiliate commission — any amount. The first dollar proves the path exists.

Phase 3 · Months 7–9 — Product + list

Job: hit 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours for full ad revenue. Start an email list (free tier is fine to 1,000 subscribers). Ship v1 of your digital product to your first few hundred subscribers — ugly, cheap, real.

Gate at month 9: $100+/month total across all three layers.

Phase 4 · Months 10–12 — Automate and compound

Job: now build the pipeline (section 08). If it genuinely halves your production time, go to 2 videos a week. If it doesn't, stay at one and put the hours into the product instead.

Gate at month 12: $500+/month, and — more importantly — a library of 48 videos that keeps earning whether or not you show up in month 13.

07

Operating rules

The plan above is ordinary. These rules are the part that decides whether you're still doing it in March.

One mode per session

Never script and edit on the same day. Switching modes costs you 20 minutes of ramp-up each time, and you only have twelve.

Publish at 80%

A video you don't publish earns $0 forever. Your video 40 will be better than your video 4 — that only happens if video 4 goes out.

Write "done" before you start

One line at the top of each session: what finished looks like. Without it you will polish until the session ends and produce nothing.

No new tools for 90 days

Tool-shopping feels exactly like progress and produces exactly zero videos. Lock your stack in week one.

One metric per month

Check analytics once a month, on Package Day, for ten minutes. Daily dashboard-checking is a slot machine with worse odds.

Novelty budget: 1 in 4

One video a month can be a weird experiment. This contains the itch inside the channel instead of it becoming a new channel.

Start with the worst part

First 10 minutes of every session: the bit you're avoiding. Momentum is easier to steer than to summon.

Tell one person the schedule

Send them your four session dates each month. External accountability does more than any productivity app you'll be tempted to buy.

The failure mode to watch for

You are technical, which means building the system will always feel better than using it. Rewriting your pipeline is procrastination wearing a lab coat. The rule in section 08 exists specifically to protect you from this, and it is the most important rule in the document.

08

The automation pipeline

Hard rule

Do not build any of this until 8 videos are published.

You cannot automate a process you haven't run manually eight times — you'll automate the wrong steps beautifully. Month 3 at the earliest.

Then build it in this order, cheapest win first:

Build1
The locked style guide
A STYLE.md + SCRIPT_TEMPLATE.md your AI assistant reads every time. Biggest quality jump, costs one hour, no code. Do this even if you build nothing else.
Build2
Transcript → packaging
One script that takes the finished transcript and emits title variants, description, chapter markers, tags, and timestamps for 2–3 Shorts. Saves ~40 min per video, every video, forever.
Build3
Idea pipeline
Pull YouTube autocomplete + related searches for your seed terms into a ranked ideas.md. Turns Script Day's hardest 30 minutes into a 5-minute pick.
Build4
Scheduled upload
YouTube Data API v3 for metadata + scheduling straight from your files. Only worth it once you're doing 8+ videos a month.
Build5
Weekly stats pull
YouTube Analytics API into one tiny dashboard, refreshed weekly. Replaces the urge to check Studio daily with one honest weekly number.
What to keep manual, permanently

Your voice. Never synthesise it — it's your entire defence against the inauthentic-content policy and the reason people subscribe.

The hook and the opinion. AI writes competent middles. It cannot write the first 15 seconds or the one honest take that makes a video worth watching. Those two things are the video.

Thumbnail final call. Generate options, but pick with your own eyes.

09

The Australian admin

You already have an ABN as a sole trader, which covers most of it. Four things left.

Do in week 1

Submit US tax info in AdSense

Google withholds US tax on the share of your earnings from US viewers. Australian residents claim treaty benefits via the W-8BEN inside AdSense, which reduces that rate substantially. Skip this form and you get withheld at the maximum default rate on worldwide earnings. It takes about ten minutes.

GST: almost certainly not yet

Registration is required once turnover reaches the $75,000/year threshold. At a $500–1k/month target you're nowhere near it — but check annually rather than assuming.

Income goes in your personal return

Sole-trader income is reported in the business section of your individual tax return — there's no separate company return. Set aside a portion of every payout for tax from the first dollar.

Separate account + receipts, day one

One bank account for this only, and keep every receipt. Your microphone, software subscriptions, and a reasonable portion of internet costs are likely deductible business expenses.

Verify before you rely on this

These are the general rules as I understand them, not tax advice, and I wasn't able to re-verify current rates and thresholds while writing this. Confirm the specifics with the ATO or an accountant before your first return — it's one cheap conversation and it covers hobby-vs-business classification too, which matters for whether you can deduct anything.

11

Reality check & exit rules

The odds, said plainly

Most channels started with these constraints do not reach $500/month in twelve months. The single strongest predictor of the ones that do isn't niche, gear, or thumbnails — it's whether all 48 videos got published. Consistency is the entire edge, which is precisely why the rhythm in section 04 matters more than every strategy note in this document.

And to be direct about the word in your question: this is not passive income for twelve months. It's a part-time job that builds an asset. The passive part starts in year two, when a library of 48 evergreen videos keeps earning ad and affiliate revenue in months where you publish nothing at all. That's real — it's just on the far side of the work, not instead of it.
