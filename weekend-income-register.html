How to read this

The composite is a weighted blend of five sub-scores, all 0–100, and the sliders above are live — drag them and the register re-ranks. Defaults are weighted the way you asked: ceiling 30, return 25, weekend fit 20, portability 15, capital 10.

  • Return — realistic money for the effort, net of costs. Not the number the platform advertises.
  • Ceiling — how high it goes and whether it compounds. Risk-adjusted, so a lottery ticket with a huge best case still scores mid.
  • Weekend fit — how well it survives being done only Saturday and Sunday.
  • Portable — how much of it you keep when you move to Melbourne.
  • Capital — how light it is on your $6k. High score means cheap to start.

Risk is deliberately outside the score, shown as its own badge, because a high-risk 70 and a low-risk 70 are not the same bet. Read the two together.

Advice that applies to all of them

Before anything else

Read your employment contract

You said keep it separate from engineering, which sidesteps most of the risk. But plenty of contracts also carry a general secondary employment or "full time and attention" clause that catches any outside work, not just competing work.

Check it once, now. If it needs consent, ask in writing and keep the reply. It is a five-minute job that protects everything below it.

Structure

Sole trader first, always

Get a free ABN and stay a sole trader. A company costs ~$600 to register plus ~$300/yr ASIC review fees and an accountant, and buys you nothing until you have real profit or real liability.

You only have to register for GST at $75k turnover. Below that, staying out of GST is a genuine 10% price advantage against the franchises — unless you are selling mostly to businesses who claim it back, in which case registering early lets you claim GST on that fusion splicer.

Tax

Your side income stacks on your salary

Side income is taxed at your marginal rate, not from zero. On an engineer's salary that is likely 37% plus Medicare, so a $1,000 weekend nets you around $600.

Put 35–40% of every dollar aside the day it lands. Claim what is genuinely yours — tools, course fees, insurance, the business share of your phone and car — and keep a logbook if you drive. Business losses may not be deductible against salary unless you pass one of the ATO's non-commercial loss tests, so don't plan around it.

Protection

Insurance before your first paid job

Public liability is roughly $400–$900/yr for a low-risk trade service and most commercial clients will not let you on site without a $10–20m certificate.

Anything where you touch a client's equipment or property also wants care, custody and control cover. And check that your car insurer knows if you are doing rideshare or delivery — a standard policy can be void during a paid trip.

The strategy

Run one cash track and one compounding track

You said a 6–12 month zero is acceptable, which is the right posture — but it is much easier to sustain when something else is paying for the gear and the courses.

The pattern that works: a service business that turns weekend hours into cash and recurring contracts, funding a content or product asset that pays nothing for a year and then pays without you. Don't run three. Two is already a lot on twelve hours.

The move

Build for the suitcase

Melbourne in 12–18 months is the quiet filter on this whole list. Anything whose value sits in local physical placement — vending sites, a dog-boarding clientele, a co-hosting portfolio — is an asset you will have to sell or abandon.

That is not automatically a reason to skip them; a vending route sells as a going concern. Just enter them knowing you have an 18-month horizon, and price the exit into the decision.

Ticket sequencing

Do the paperwork while employed

Courses, registrations and supervised hours are far easier to accumulate with a stable salary and no pressure to earn. The ACMA open registration in particular needs 360 hours of practical cabling experience signed off before full registration — that is the long pole, and it is a clock you want started early.

Same logic for the test and tag competency. It is one day and a few hundred dollars, and it never expires on you.

Gear discipline

Hire until the work is proven

Your $6k is the scarcest thing you have and the fastest way to lose it is buying capability you have not yet sold. Fusion splicers hire. So do OTDRs, trailers, pressure washers and vans.

The rule: buy the tool only after the work it does has paid for it once. The one exception is cheap consumable kit that gets you to a first paying job — a PAT tester is $1,200 and pays for itself inside three site visits.

Deliberately left out

The highest-paying options on the board, excluded at your request

You asked to keep this separate from the day job, so the engineering-leverage family is not in the register. For the record, this is what that decision costs: freelance electrical design and drafting ($90–160/hr), solar and energy consulting, compliance auditing, expert reports, and sessional TAFE or RTO teaching ($60–110/hr). Every one of them beats the top of this list on hourly rate, needs almost no capital, and is entirely weekend-shaped.

They are excluded for good reasons — conflict of interest, contract clauses, and the fact that you probably do not want your weekend to feel like your week. But if the contract check comes back clean and you ever want the money more than the separation, that is where it is. Sessional teaching in particular is the mildest version: it is adjacent to your expertise without competing with your employer, and it ports to Victoria intact.

Also left out entirely: anything requiring an A-grade electrical licence, since you hold the degree and not the trade licence. Note that this does not block test and tag — that one is competency-based, which is a large part of why it ranks where it does.
